# **IRON FRONTIER: RAILS & RAIDERS**

### A Wild West train-management roguelite about trade, survival, route planning, and automated convoy combat.

---

# 1. High Concept

You operate a small independent steam train during a fictionalized Wild West railway boom.

Each run begins with:

* One weak locomotive
* A few carriages
* A small crew
* Limited supplies
* A company charter
* A procedurally generated railway map

You must cross the frontier through multiple regions, traveling from station to station while:

* Buying and selling goods
* Completing contracts
* Recruiting crew
* Transporting workers and passengers
* Reconfiguring and upgrading the train
* Managing coal, water, food, ammunition, morale, and repairs
* Choosing between safe and profitable routes
* Surviving raiders, rival companies, disasters, and mechanical failures

Combat is automated.

The player does not aim guns or move individual characters during battle. Instead, the outcome depends on:

* Train composition
* Carriage order
* Crew assignments
* Weapons and armor
* Resources
* Scouting
* Combat doctrine
* Emergency commands
* Decisions made earlier in the run

The central fantasy is:

> **My train is a growing frontier company, merchant caravan, home, and fortress moving through a hostile railway network.**

---

# 2. Roguelite Run Structure

The entire game is designed around repeatable runs rather than a permanent open-ended campaign.

## Typical Run Length

A complete run lasts approximately:

* **90–150 minutes** on normal speed
* Shorter runs available through compact maps
* Longer challenge runs available through difficulty modifiers

## Run Structure

Each run contains four stages:

1. **The Plains**
2. **The Badlands**
3. **The Mountain Frontier**
4. **The Final Railhead**

Each region contains:

* Several station nodes
* Optional branches
* Dangerous shortcuts
* Special landmarks
* One regional crisis
* One regional boss or major obstacle

The player does not visit every location.

Choosing one route permanently closes other opportunities, forcing commitment and making each run different.

---

# 3. Run Objective

Every run has one primary **Company Charter**.

The charter changes the player’s starting conditions, preferred strategy, scoring, and final challenge.

## Frontier Merchant Charter

Reach the final railhead with a target company value.

Strengths:

* Better market information
* Larger starting cargo capacity
* Improved trade prices

Challenges:

* Weak starting defenses
* Higher rival-company pressure

---

## Continental Mail Charter

Deliver official mail through every region before the deadline.

Strengths:

* Faster starting locomotive
* Reduced station service time
* Reliable contract income

Challenges:

* Strict deadlines
* Penalties for excessive detours
* Limited train weight

---

## Railway Construction Charter

Transport workers and materials to complete an unfinished line.

Strengths:

* Better repair abilities
* Reduced construction-material prices
* Worker groups can assist during emergencies

Challenges:

* Heavy cargo
* High fuel usage
* Frequent track hazards

---

## Frontier Relief Charter

Deliver medicine, food, and civilians through unstable territory.

Strengths:

* Medical car access
* Higher settlement rewards
* Strong reputation growth

Challenges:

* Fragile passengers
* Perishable cargo
* Moral pressure during events

---

## Marshal’s Special Charter

Break the power of a major outlaw gang.

Strengths:

* Starting guard crew
* Bounty rewards
* Better weapon access

Challenges:

* More combat
* Greater ammunition consumption
* Outlaws become stronger throughout the run

---

## Black Route Charter

Operate as a smuggler between outlaw-controlled settlements.

Strengths:

* Hidden compartments
* Black-market access
* High-profit illegal cargo

Challenges:

* Law inspections
* Unreliable clients
* Betrayal and confiscation risks

---

# 4. Victory and Defeat

## Victory

The player wins by reaching the final railhead and completing the charter objective.

Examples:

* Reach a required company value
* Deliver a protected cargo
* Complete the unfinished railway
* Defeat the outlaw war train
* Arrive before winter closes the route
* Transport a required number of workers or passengers

The final score considers:

* Remaining money
* Train value
* Crew survivors
* Contracts completed
* Settlements assisted
* Reputation
* Cargo delivered
* Time remaining
* Damage sustained
* Difficulty modifiers

---

## Defeat

A run ends when:

* The locomotive is destroyed
* The company becomes bankrupt
* The charter deadline is missed
* The required cargo or passenger is lost
* The crew mutinies
* The train becomes stranded without fuel, water, or recovery options
* The player surrenders the company to a rival corporation

Most failures before the final region should be gradual rather than immediate.

A bad encounter may cause:

* Stolen cargo
* Injured crew
* Lost time
* Damaged carriages
* Debt
* Reduced morale
* Forced route changes

This creates recovery stories instead of allowing every mistake to end the run instantly.

---

# 5. Core Gameplay Loop

The repeating loop is:

> **Station → Prepare → Choose Route → Travel → Resolve Events → Survive Encounter → Arrive → Upgrade**

---

## Phase 1: Station

At a station, the player can:

* Buy and sell goods
* Accept contracts
* Recruit crew
* Transport workers and passengers
* Repair the train
* Purchase carriages
* Upgrade equipment
* Reorganize carriage order
* Refill coal, water, food, ammunition, and medical supplies
* Gather rumors
* Use local services
* Negotiate with factions
* Pay wages and debts

The player cannot do everything at every station.

Each station has limited:

* Inventory
* Crew candidates
* Repair quality
* Upgrade options
* Time
* Services

Remaining too long may cause:

* Contract deadlines to tighten
* Markets to change
* Rivals to advance
* Weather conditions to worsen
* Outlaws to strengthen

---

## Phase 2: Route Planning

The player chooses one of several connected railway paths.

Every path has visible and hidden properties.

### Visible Information

* Distance
* Estimated travel time
* Terrain
* Coal requirement
* Water requirement
* Known danger
* Track condition
* Tolls
* Weather forecast
* Destination type
* Weight or length restrictions

### Hidden Information

* Raider activity
* Ambush locations
* Track sabotage
* Special events
* Secret merchants
* Rival movements
* Cargo opportunities
* Weather shifts
* Faction patrols

Scouts, rumors, telegraph operators, and previous discoveries reveal hidden information.

Information becomes a resource.

---

## Phase 3: Train Preparation

Before departure, the player determines:

* Carriage order
* Crew positions
* Speed policy
* Resource reserves
* Protected cargo
* Combat doctrine
* Repair priority
* Medical priority
* Emergency cargo to sacrifice
* Whether to travel during day or night

This is the main strategic preparation phase.

---

## Phase 4: Journey

The train moves automatically along the selected route.

During travel:

* Coal and water are consumed
* Crew members become tired
* Cargo may deteriorate
* Mechanical wear increases
* Weather changes
* Passengers create events
* Raiders may appear
* Track hazards may interrupt progress
* Optional opportunities may appear

The player handles decisions but does not manually drive the locomotive.

---

## Phase 5: Encounter

Possible encounters include:

* Raider attack
* Rival train
* Broken bridge
* Damaged track
* Law inspection
* Stranded passengers
* Flood
* Rockslide
* Fire
* Labor dispute
* Disease
* Abandoned depot
* Derailment risk
* Outlaw toll
* Merchant caravan
* Construction camp

Some encounters are narrative choices.

Others use the automated combat or hazard-resolution system.

---

## Phase 6: Arrival

At the destination, the game calculates:

* Profit
* Contract completion
* Cargo condition
* Crew injuries
* Train damage
* Reputation changes
* Settlement changes
* New opportunities
* Rival progress
* Regional threat escalation

The loop then repeats.

---

# 10. Carriage Order

Carriage position affects:

* Defense
* Fire spread
* Boarding
* Repairs
* Weapon arcs
* Passenger safety
* Cargo theft
* Collision damage
* Emergency detachment

Examples:

* A safe car placed beside a guard car is better protected.
* A workshop near the locomotive repairs engine damage faster.
* Explosives near the tender create catastrophic fire risk.
* A passenger car near the rear is vulnerable to boarding.
* A Gatling car may only cover one or two directions.
* Heavy cars placed poorly reduce handling on steep routes.

The player may rearrange the train only at stations with sufficient railway-yard facilities.

This prevents constant risk-free reconfiguration.

# 15. Dynamic Market Rules

To stop simple trade loops:

* Selling large quantities reduces local price.
* Stations have limited money and demand.
* Market shortages can be fulfilled.
* Rivals can reach stations first.
* Goods deteriorate.
* Travel consumes time and resources.
* Carriage capacity is limited.
* Heavy cargo increases operating costs.
* News may become outdated.
* Some routes close after being used.
* Regional crises alter prices.

The best cargo is therefore dependent on:

* Current route
* Train build
* Available time
* Threat level
* Destination demand
* Charter objective

---

# 17. Settlement Development During a Run

The player can influence stations, but the game does not become a permanent city-building campaign.

Each station has a simple development state:

1. Struggling
2. Stable
3. Growing
4. Prosperous

Delivering needed goods or workers may improve it.

A developed station can gain:

* Better repairs
* New market goods
* More crew candidates
* Telegraph services
* Safer nearby routes
* New connections
* Better contract rewards

A neglected station may:

* Fall under outlaw control
* Close
* Lose services
* Create refugees
* Increase regional danger

Settlement development lasts only for the current run.

This creates meaningful world impact without undermining the roguelite structure.

# 21. Automated Combat

Combat should feel like a visible test of the player’s preparation.

It should not feel like:

> The game secretly rolled a number and declared a winner.

The player must understand why each result occurred.

---

## Combat Stage 1: Detection

The game checks:

* Scout ability
* Caboose
* Terrain
* Weather
* Time of day
* Train noise
* Enemy concealment
* Telegraph intelligence

Early detection provides preparation time.

A failed detection creates an ambush.

---

## Combat Stage 2: Response Choice

Before combat begins, the player chooses a response.

* Fight
* Outrun
* Negotiate
* Bribe
* Intimidate
* Hide
* Call for help
* Surrender selected cargo
* Deploy decoy cargo

Not every option is always available.

---

## Combat Stage 3: Doctrine

When fighting or escaping, the player selects one doctrine.

### Protect the Engine

Crew prioritizes locomotive survival.

### Protect Cargo

Guards defend valuable freight.

### Protect Passengers

Crew minimizes passenger casualties.

### Aggressive Defense

High ammunition use and increased enemy casualties.

### Hold Positions

Crew avoids risky movement between cars.

### Escape

The engineer and mechanic prioritize speed.

### Anti-Boarding

Crew focuses on riders reaching the train.

### Break the Blockade

The engine prepares to ram through obstacles.

---

## Combat Stage 4: Automated Resolution

The battle unfolds across several phases.

### Chase

Enemies attempt to reach the train.

Relevant factors:

* Speed
* Weight
* Engine condition
* Terrain
* Weather

### Ranged Attack

Guards and mounted weapons exchange fire.

Relevant factors:

* Weapon range
* Crew skill
* Cover
* Armor
* Ammunition
* Visibility

### Boarding

Enemies climb onto exposed cars.

Relevant factors:

* Carriage order
* Guard placement
* Doors
* Barbed wire
* Train speed
* Crew morale

### Sabotage

Enemies target:

* Brakes
* Couplings
* Cargo locks
* Engine
* Fuel
* Passengers

### Withdrawal

Enemies flee, the train escapes, or one side becomes unable to continue.

---

# 22. Emergency Commands

The player receives a small number of command points during battle.

These are high-level interventions, not direct unit control.

Commands include:

* **Full Steam**
* **Brace for Impact**
* **Focus Left**
* **Focus Right**
* **Protect the Engine**
* **Extinguish Fire**
* **Send the Mechanic**
* **Release Decoy Cargo**

Command points are limited.

# 25. Rival Company System

One rival company is generated at the beginning of each run.

The rival has:

* A charter
* A locomotive
* A strategy
* A reputation
* A company leader
* Its own route across the map

Possible rival strategies:

* Trade dominance
* Contract racing
* Sabotage
* Corporate influence
* Military force
* Smuggling

The rival can:

* Reach stations before the player
* Buy limited cargo
* Complete contracts
* Raise or lower prices
* Influence factions
* Block routes
* Attack the player indirectly
* Become a temporary ally

The rival grows during the run.

Ignoring it may allow it to become the final boss.

---

# 26. Regional Escalation

Each region contains a threat meter.

Threat increases when:

* The player carries valuable cargo
* Outlaws are attacked
* Contracts fail
* The rival gains influence
* Time passes
* The player repeatedly uses the same safe routes

Higher threat causes:

* Stronger enemies
* More inspections
* Higher tolls
* More sabotage
* More dangerous events

This prevents the player from safely farming one region indefinitely.

# 27. Run Progression

During a run, progression comes through:

* Money
* Better crew
* Train upgrades
* Carriages
* Rare blueprints
* Reputation
* Settlement services
* Route information
* Charter milestones

The player’s train should visibly change throughout the run.

Early run:

> Small locomotive, one boxcar, weak crew.

Mid-run:

> Specialized train with a clear economic or defensive identity.

Late run:

> Powerful but expensive machine carrying accumulated damage and difficult obligations.

---

# 28. Meta-Progression Between Runs

Permanent progression should unlock variety rather than directly remove difficulty.

## Good Permanent Unlocks

* New locomotive types
* New carriages
* New charter types
* New crew backgrounds
* New factions
* New events
* New regions
* New bosses
* New cargo categories
* New blueprints
* Challenge modifiers
* Cosmetic train designs

## Limited Starting Advantages

Some small starting bonuses are acceptable:

* One additional crew candidate
* Slightly better starting market information
* One basic blueprint choice
* A small emergency supply

Avoid permanent systems that simply provide:

* Large damage bonuses
* Huge starting money
* Constantly cheaper goods
* Permanent enemy reduction

The player should improve mainly through knowledge and unlocked options.

---

# 29. Blueprint System

Rare upgrades appear during runs.

A blueprint gives the player a choice between several train-defining technologies.

Examples:

## Condensing Boiler

Reduces water consumption but lowers maximum speed.

## Reinforced Cowcatcher

Improves blockade breaking but adds weight.

## Hidden Safe Compartments

Protects valuables but reduces cargo capacity.

## Steam-Powered Gatling Mount

High firepower but consumes coal and ammunition during battle.

## Emergency Coupling Release

Allows one carriage to be detached instantly.

## Mobile Telegraph Array

Reveals future route conditions but requires a telegraph operator.

Blueprints encourage different builds and improve replayability.

---

# 30. Anti-Snowball Systems

A successful player should become stronger, but not untouchable.

## Increasing Upkeep

Larger trains consume more:

* Fuel
* Water
* Wages
* Repair parts

## Threat Attraction

Valuable trains attract stronger enemies.

## Route Restrictions

Heavy trains cannot use every bridge or mountain route.

## Limited Crew

More carriages require more workers, but crew capacity remains constrained.

## Rising Repair Costs

Advanced equipment requires better stations and rarer parts.

## Rival Scaling

The rival grows when the player becomes wealthy or delays excessively.

## Reputation Tradeoffs

Helping one faction may anger another.

These systems ensure that expansion creates new problems.

---

# 31. Recovery Systems

To prevent one mistake from making the rest of the run hopeless, the player may use emergency recovery options.

* Sell a carriage
* Take a loan
* Pawn valuable cargo
* Reduce crew wages
* Accept an exploitative corporate contract
* Request towing
* Abandon cargo
* Surrender to outlaws
* Work for a station
* Replace an injured crew member
* Detour to a repair yard

Recovery should be costly and create future consequences.

---

# 32. Difficulty System

Difficulty can be built using modular modifiers.

Examples:

* Scarce water
* Expensive fuel
* Stronger raiders
* Hidden route danger
* Faster rival growth
* Harsher weather
* Higher crew wages
* Limited station repairs
* Permanent crew death
* No emergency loans
* Increased cargo deterioration

The hardest difficulty should test mastery without simply multiplying enemy health.

---

# 33. Narrative Structure

The game does not require a fixed linear story.

Narrative emerges through:

* Crew members
* Rival companies
* Regional crises
* Passengers
* Settlements
* Faction choices
* Run outcomes

Each run generates a frontier story.

Example:

> The player begins as a merchant, accepts a medical contract, becomes involved in a mining strike, loses a cargo car to raiders, takes a corporate loan, then reaches the final railhead while trying to escape the corporation now claiming ownership of the train.

---

# 34. Visual Presentation

Recommended format:

* Stylized 3D or detailed 2.5D
* Side-view train during travel and combat
* Illustrated map screen
* Physical carriages visibly changing
* Strong silhouettes
* Dust, steam, snow, fire, and smoke
* Clear weapon arcs and crew movement

Combat should clearly show:

* Where enemies are attacking
* Which carriage is targeted
* Which crew member is responding
* Why an ability activated
* Why damage occurred

---

# 35. Combat Report

After every battle, the player receives a clear explanation.

Example:

## Minor Victory

* Raiders detected early by the scout.
* Rear guard car prevented boarding.
* Train speed was too low to escape.
* Ammunition ran out during the final phase.
* One boxcar was breached.
* Medicine cargo remained protected.
* Mechanic prevented engine failure.

The report should explicitly teach the player.

It should never simply display:

> Victory chance: 63%.

---

# 36. Example Run

The player begins with:

* Small freight locomotive
* Tender
* Boxcar
* Passenger car
* Engineer
* Conductor
* One guard
* 150 dollars

The selected charter is **Railway Construction**.

The player must deliver workers and rail materials to the final mountain railhead.

At the first farming town, the player:

* Buys grain
* Accepts a mail contract
* Hires a mechanic
* Learns that a mining camp has a tool shortage

The player chooses a dangerous shortcut.

During the journey:

* Heavy rain damages the track
* The mechanic prevents a wheel failure
* A stranded surveyor joins the train
* The surveyor reveals a hidden branch on the next map

At the mining camp:

* Grain is sold at high profit
* Tools are purchased
* A worker group is taken aboard
* A workshop car becomes available

Later, the train reaches the Badlands carrying:

* Workers
* Tools
* Medicine
* A valuable payroll contract

The player has expanded into:

> Locomotive → Tender → Guard Car → Worker Car → Boxcar → Workshop → Caboose

A large gang attacks in a canyon.

The player selects:

* Protect Cargo doctrine
* Rear guard assignment
* Cautious ammunition policy

The raiders cannot reach the payroll car, but they damage the locomotive.

The player uses an emergency command to detach the rear boxcar after it catches fire.

The train survives, but loses part of its cargo capacity.

The player must then choose:

* Continue toward the objective with less cargo
* Take a loan at a corporate station
* Detour to purchase another car
* Abandon the payroll contract
* Sell the workshop car

The rest of the run changes because of that battle.

That is the intended roguelite experience.

# 37. MVP Scope

The first playable version should focus only on the essential loop.

## MVP Content

* One procedural region
* 15–20 nodes
* Three station types
* Two locomotive types
* Six carriage types
* Eight goods
* Five crew roles
* Three contract types
* Three enemy types
* Twenty events
* Four combat doctrines
* Five emergency commands
* One rival company
* One regional boss
* One charter
* 45–75 minute run

## Essential Systems

1. Procedural route map
2. Trading
3. Coal and water
4. Carriage purchase and ordering
5. Crew assignment
6. Route attributes
7. Events
8. Automated combat
9. Persistent damage
10. Run victory and defeat
11. Basic unlock progression

Do not initially build:

* Complex faction politics
* Multiple seasons
* Large relationship systems
* Dozens of cargo types
* Full settlement simulation
* Multiple maps
* Large narrative campaigns

The MVP must first prove that this loop is enjoyable:

> **Buy → Prepare → Choose Route → Travel → Survive → Profit → Upgrade**

---

# 38. Core Design Rules

## Every Route Must Create a Tradeoff

No route should be universally best.

## Preparation Must Decide Combat

The player should understand that victory came from their build and decisions.

## Expansion Must Create New Risks

A larger train gains power but becomes expensive, slow, and attractive to enemies.

## Loss Must Usually Be Partial

Most failures should create difficult recovery situations rather than instantly ending the run.

## The Train Must Tell the Story

By the end of a run, the train’s layout, damage, crew, and cargo should represent everything that happened.

## Trading Must Affect More Than Money

Cargo should influence events, settlements, contracts, combat risk, and route options.

---

# 39. Final Game Identity

**Iron Frontier: Rails & Raiders** is a train-building, trading, and survival roguelite in which the player crosses a procedurally generated Wild West frontier.

Its identity comes from six connected systems:

1. **The railway map is the roguelite path.**
2. **The train is the player’s build.**
3. **Cargo creates both profit and danger.**
4. **Crew assignments determine how the train functions.**
5. **Automated combat tests preparation rather than reflexes.**
6. **Every run creates a different frontier journey.**

The final player fantasy is:

> **I began with a weak locomotive and a few desperate workers. I ended the run commanding a damaged, overloaded, heavily armed frontier company that barely reached the final station alive.**
